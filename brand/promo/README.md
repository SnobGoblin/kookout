# Kookout promo: cartoon

A 9:16 social video, 30 s (cut to the narration), for Instagram/Facebook wing foil and surf groups and later the
kookout.no landing page. Cartoon only: **2D, in a 1970s surf-poster screen-print style** (being redone; see Status),
with a laid-back surfer narrator.

The message: **Kookout is the tap on the shoulder, not another forecast site.** It tells you when and where your
spots are good, so you don't have to remember to check. The details (wind maps, stations, ships, webcams) stay with
the sites riders already use and like, such as Foilmenyen. The video must never make those look bad: the joke is
never about the tools: Gary *loves* his charts and the narrator respects them ("Respect."); his only problem is
that he also has a job. Kookout tells him when it's worth opening them. (All the metrics in his scene are invented.)

**Post only once Kookout 1.1 is in the App Store**: the Lock Screen widget (scene 4) comes with 1.1.

## 2. Voice-over

The register: a young Californian surfer, slow and lazy, warm and quietly stoked, and **never quite sure of
anything** (phrases trail off with a half-amused questioning lilt). Light surf slang ("bro", "condish", "sesh"),
at most one per line; never a real person's catchphrases.

The voice is saved in ElevenLabs as **"Kookout narrator 2"** (Voice Design, then remixed at low strength from
"Kookout narrator").
Never clone or imitate a real person; if we ever want a known voice, we hire that person. Write pauses with full
stops and commas, never a stand-alone "…" (ElevenLabs may voice it as a syllable). Slow lines down with Text to
Speech's speed slider rather than by rewriting them.

## 3. Character and shot prompts (2D)

Rules for Gemini (learnt the hard way):
1. A **new chat** per image or shot. Never mention the app, an ad or a promo; never "extend" an old clip (it burns in
   titles). Say "no text, letters, numbers, logos" every time, and for flags "plain, single colour".
2. Still first, check it, then "Animate this image as the first frame. Camera still." Gemini ignores lengths
   (always 10 s, 720 × 1280, 24 fps); I cut what I need.
3. Problems are fixed by regenerating, **never by cropping** the shot.
4. Things that overlap in the still (two wings, a paw on someone else's wing) get their depth order wrong. Compose
   with open space between characters. Check every overlap close up before animating, and in the animation check
   every eighth of a second where they pass.
5. Ten variations in one grid is a cheap way to decide a style; Gemini's numbering may drift from the prompt.
6. Phone screens, notifications, metric cards, the widget and all captions are drawn by `promo.swift`, never
   generated (AI garbles text). Real sites stay generic (no real logos or names).

Style: attach `generated/style-6-3.jpg` and start with:

> The first attached image shows the exact style: a 1970s surf-poster screen print with bold, even black ink
> outlines, flat colour areas, light print texture and a limited palette. [Then the character sheet(s) as
> references for the characters only.] Vertical 9:16, no frame or border. No text, letters, numbers, logos or
> captions anywhere.

Characters (Norwegian coast animals; both likeable, Gary is never the loser):
1. **Kai**, a chill sea otter: chubby, light brown fur, cream face, small whiskers, orange knit beanie with a
   pompom, silver-framed sunglasses, relaxed half-smile. 2D sheet: `generated/kai-2d.jpg` (3D: `kai-master.jpg`).
2. **Gary**, an eager Atlantic puffin: black and white, striped orange beak, red eye rings, wide eager eyes, light
   blue shirt and navy tie. 2D sheet: to make, from `style-6-3.jpg` and `gary-master.png`:

```text
The first attached image shows the exact style: a 1970s surf-poster screen print with bold, even black ink outlines, flat colour areas, light print texture and a limited palette. The second image is only a reference for the character.

Make a character sheet of this Atlantic puffin in that exact style, on plain cream paper: black and white feathers, the striped orange beak, red eye rings, wide eager eyes, a light blue shirt and a navy tie.
Show him five times in a row, full body, same size: front view, three-quarter view, side view facing right, sitting at a laptop looking delighted, and standing holding a coffee mug, looking at his phone.
No background scenery, no frame or border. No text, letters, labels, numbers, logos or captions anywhere.
```

Riding pose, to paste into every riding prompt (the most common error; riders notice):

```text
Riding pose: standing sideways to the direction of travel like a surfer, front foot near the mast, back foot behind, knees bent and hips low. Both hands on the wing's two handles, front hand forward, back hand behind, arms almost straight. The wing is up in front of and above the rider: a fat inflated tube bent into an arc as the leading edge (arc forward and up), a straight strut down the middle with the handles on it, thin canopy between them, filled with wind. The rider leans back against the pull of the wing. The board flies above the water on a thin mast, the foil under the surface.
```

A wave is the back hand off the handle; the front hand keeps holding.

## 4. Assembly

`promo.swift` (run from the repo root: `swift -O brand/promo.swift [check folder]`, or `--only <scene>`) takes the
shots from `generated/` ("plates", with `from`, `length`, `cue`, optional `speed` and a tracked phone `anchor`),
the voice takes from `vo/`, draws the captions, phone screens, metric cards, widget and end card, adds the swooshes,
grain (`grain`) and optionally holds frames on twos (`onTwos`), and writes `kookout-cartoon.mp4` (1080 × 1920,
30 fps). A missing shot falls back to my code-drawn version of the scene. The check folder gets PNGs with
Instagram's covered areas shaded red: top 250 px, bottom 450 px, right 140 px (between 450 and 1100 px up).

Helpers in `tools/` (run with `swift`):
1. `frames.swift <video> <folder> [step] [from]`: a PNG every 0.5 s (or `step`), to check a generated shot.
2. `sheet.swift <out.png> <frames…>`: a contact sheet, 5 across.
3. `segments.swift <audio>`: where the words are in a voice take, for timing.

`sips` crops with `--cropOffset <y> <x>` (y first, and 0 0 means centred: use 1 1 for the corner).
